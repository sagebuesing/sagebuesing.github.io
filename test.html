Physics is the human attempt to explain our world.  The success of that attempt is evident in the technology of our society.  We are surrounded by the products resulting from the application of that understanding, from clocks to medicine to computers.  You have already developed your own physical perception of the world around you.  Some of those ideas are consistent with the accepted theories of physics while others are not. This laboratory is designed to focus your attention on your interactions with the world so that you can recognize where your ideas agree with those accepted by physics and where they do not. 

The three components of the course - lecture, discussion, and laboratory - each serve a different purpose.  You are presented with physics theories in lecture and in your textbook. The laboratory is where you apply those theories to problems in the real world.  

The amount you learn in lab will depend on the time you spend in preparation before working through the lab.  Before starting the lab each week you must read the appropriate sections of your text, other suggested readings, and work through the lab warm-up to develop a fairly clear idea of what will be happening.

Most lab experiment modules follow this four part sequence: 

              • Before You Start
              • Warm up and Prediction
              • Exploration, Measurement and Analysis
              • Conclusion

The Before You Start page introduces the lab problem within a made-up context.  The page has equipment notes, experimental setup pictures, tips and also lists suggested readings to complete.  You are then assigned a series of Warm-up questions based on the problem that you will work through to help you form your Prediction.  It is expected that you complete the readings and submit your Warm-up questions and prediction before you start working on the lab.  

Using your prediction you will experiment with the lab setup used to model the problem, performing an Exploration of the problem and equipment.  Through your exploration, you should form a Measurement plan to collect the necessary data.  Before you finish with the lab exercise you will complete a TA Check and submit and .xls file of any generated graphs and tables you generated during the Analysis of your collected data.  For the Conclusion you will answer questions related to the context presented in the introduction. 

Please do not hesitate to discuss any difficulties you have understanding the material or applying your knowledge with your lab instructor.

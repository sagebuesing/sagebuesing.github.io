## Course Structure

### Discussion

Discussions meet on Tuesdays. For our discussions, we will be working through a problem in small groups every week. For section 202 we will meet in Tate B85 at 7:15PM-8:05PM, and for Section 232 we will meet in Ford 155 at 8:15PM-9:05PM.
Generally I like to kick everyone off with a little bit of background information on the problem, and give everyone 20 or so minutes to work. I will be available at any point throughout these normal discussions to ask questions, so please do.
Asking questions is an important aspect of learning, so please don't feel embarrassed to ask me anything you might be unsure of in your problem solving process. After those first 20 minutes, I often like to go through the problem as a whole class, and maybe do some of the problem on the board with everyone.
Again, please be willing to share your questions with the rest of the class. If you have a question about something, it's likely that a few other students do as well!
I will give you some more time to finish the problem, then likely take the last 10 minutes of class to give a solution on the board.

Discussions will include group quizzes with your group, so it will pay to get to know them (and how to work with them) well. Group quizzes will take place on the weeks of the main class's quizzes. Each group will submit a combined answer to be graded.

### Lab

For labs, we will be following the lab handouts provided on Canvas. We will meet in Tate B76 at 4:40PM-6:35PM. Section 203 meets Mondays, 233 on Wednesdays. Thus the first lab for 233 is this Wednesday, and the first for 203 will be this upcoming Monday.
Note that labs (after the first one) include _pre-lab_ questions, which must be completed _before_ the lab meeting time. I will tentatively be expecting pre-lab submissions at noon on the day of the lab, though this may be subject to change later in the semester.

These lab handouts will describe the purpose and procedure for an experiment, and will ask you questions to guide you in synthesizing the data and seeing what it means. We will be using spreadsheet software to perform data analysis, so if you are not familiar with such software, I will be willing to give some introduction at the beginning of the semester. These data spreadsheets will be a core part of your submission for each lab.
It is worth taking notes on each of your labs beyond just your submission materials, as this will make your future lab report easier. Without these notes it is easy to forget details about the lab when you are writing the report potentially up to a month later.

#### Lab reports

There will be three lab reports assigned throughout the semester, occurring around the times of the three quizzes. These lab reports are expected to be between 3-5 pages (single-spaced), and will be comprised with the data analysis, and results from your lab (described using your understanding of the underlying physics involved), as well as a technical report style prose as would be seen in an academic paper. This basically means an explanation of your hypothesis and the theory around it, as well as a paper abstract, and synthesized introductions and conclusions.

The first of these lab reports will allow for a re-write, meaning that I will give you feedback on your first submission, then you will be given an additional week to submit a final draft in response to my comments. This first draft will not on its own be a part of your final grade in the class (unless you fail to submit a new final draft).

## Policies

I am pretty flexible so long as you communicate with me honestly and pre-emptively. Please don't tell me you're going to be late/absent 10 minutes before, that's rude not only to me but to your groupmates in discussions and labs.

Late work will follow a similar pattern: if you communicate with me ahead of time, we will likely be able to work something out. But I do want that in writing/email as well, to ensure I remember.

Cheating/inappropriate AI used will _not_ be tolerated. I am coming here assuming you are intending to learn the material. Cheating is not only damaging to your own learning, but also very likely damaging to your academic standing. Last year we caught quite a few students cheating who didn't even know we had caught them in the moment. A cheating mark on your transcript is far far worse than a fail. It's not worth it.

### AI

AI use should be minimal in this course. AI can generate a problem for you to solve, or give you a solution to a practice problem. This is an acceptable use for the class, however note that I have encountered students who have been confused even further by incorrect or misleading AI solutions to these practice problems. Use them at your own risk!

AI use to solve a problem that is assigned for points will be heavily scrutinized. AI can be used (again at your own risk) to help your understanding of the foundational principles of a problem, but any requests for AI to solve even a part of a problem for you will be penalized. This is my own policy, and I will not be afraid to enforce it if I see problematic behaviors.
Even considering the potential benefits of AI in the field of physics, it cannot be used effectively by people who do not themselves understand the concepts. Learning how to solve these problems without the crutch of AI is an essential part of the learning process.
